1. The board and its parts

The board is one microcontroller plus several separate chips. Each chip has its own memory and its own link to the microcontroller. Most hardware bugs are "wrong chip, wrong link, or wrong assumption about who holds the data".

The real board

Photo of the board seen from the electronics side (the screen is the faded rectangle behind it). The glass screen is on the other side.

  1. RST button (silkscreen): resets the RP2040.
  2. BOOT button (silkscreen): hold while plugging in USB to get the UF2 drive for flashing.
  3. PWR button (silkscreen): power switch.
  4. RP2040: square chip with the Raspberry Pi logo.
  5. 8-pin chip beside the buttons: probably the external flash (inferred from shape and RP2040 needing one; not confirmed by a source).
  6. Display connector: white latch, the flat cable from the screen goes in here.
  7. USB-C port: power, programming, serial.
  8. Buzzer: the round component next to USB-C.
  9. RTC 2-pin header (silkscreen): backup battery for the clock.
  10. BAT header (silkscreen, + / - marked): lithium cell for the charger.

Not marked: the IMU (QMI8658) and the touch controller. No source I found shows where they sit, and I will not guess. Touch is probably driven through the screen's flat cable. When the board arrives, look for a tiny (about 3 mm) square chip and tell me its position.

Two copies of the image

Your program draws into a framebuffer in RP2040 SRAM. Sending it over SPI copies it into the ST7789's own frame memory. The panel is refreshed from that memory, not from yours. So the image can stay on screen while your program does nothing, and you only need to send when something changes.

Two kinds of link

SPI: clock + data lines, no addresses, one device selected by a CS wire. Fast; used for pixels.
I2C: two shared wires, each device has a 7-bit address. Slower; used for small reads like touch coordinates.

Unresolved

Flash size: Waveshare's wiki says 4 MB, its product specification says 16 MB. Check on the board with os.statvfs('/') after installing MicroPython (the filesystem is smaller than the chip).

Check

The panel refreshes from which memory?

The ST7789's frame memory. Your framebuffer is a staging copy.

Where is the program stored when power is off?

SRAM loses its contents without power. The RP2040 has no on-chip flash.

Which link carries touch coordinates?

I2C, device address 0x15 in the vendor driver.
