# MacBook mounting and printable mechanics

## Exact MacBook geometry and available CAD

### Takeaway
Apple publishes the 14-inch M4 MacBook Pro's overall envelope, not a lid-only drawing or service CAD. In the bounded search completed 2026-10-27, no official A3112/Mac16,1 STEP or dimensioned lid drawing was located; the existing CAD's lid measurements remain placeholders and must not be presented as exact fit.

### Cited Findings
- Apple identifies the machine as the 2024 14-inch M4 MacBook Pro and publishes its overall width (312.6 mm), depth (221.2 mm), and closed height (15.5 mm), along with a 14.2-inch display and 3024 × 1964 resolution. These are product dimensions/specs, not separate lid thickness, corner radius, rear curvature, bezel or camera coordinates. [Apple technical specifications](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/121552)
- A3112 maps to Mac16,1 for the 14-inch 2024 base M4 model; this identification is corroborated by the iFixit device page. [iFixit model page](https://www.ifixit.com/Device/MacBook_Pro_14%22_Late_2024_M4)
- Apple's public repair manual has a display replacement procedure and exploded/parts views, but its public contents do not offer dimensioned external lid geometry or a downloadable CAD model. [Apple M4 repair manual](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/120922)
- Apple's accessory page points accessory developers to its Accessory Design Guidelines, but the public page does not itself publish the MacBook lid's mechanical drawing. [Apple accessory design resources](https://developer.apple.com/accessories/)
- Required step.parts catalog lookup was performed against the reachable API for `MacBook`, `Apple`, and `Mac16,1`; each returned `total: 0` from the catalog of 16,847 records. This is a catalog miss, not proof that no such model exists elsewhere. [step.parts API search: MacBook](https://api.step.parts/v1/parts?q=MacBook), [Apple](https://api.step.parts/v1/parts?q=Apple), [Mac16,1](https://api.step.parts/v1/parts?q=Mac16%2C1)
- A GitHub community project offers a procedural Three.js MacBook Pro model that says its measurements trace to Apple's published specs. It is not an Apple-issued engineering model and its repo page does not establish model accuracy for rear lid attachment details; treat only as visual context, not as fit geometry. No STEP model or usable license evidence was established in this bounded search. [Community Three.js model](https://github.com/AnubhavChaturvedi-GitHub/macbook-pro-threejs)
- The project README itself labels 214 mm lid height, 4.5 mm lid thickness, corner radii, display/camera placement and 1 mm adhesive/interface gap as assumptions, not measurements. [Current concept README](../../cad/peeking-concepts/README.md)

### Inferences
- Preserve the published overall envelope for visual scale only. Do not build or describe a device-facing retention surface until dimensions are taken from the actual laptop or a reliable 1:1 fit gauge.
- A scan or community mesh derived only from the outer specification cannot establish local lid thickness or rear surface offsets. The cheapest route to useful fit is a small physical gauge made against the actual machine, unless an authoritative dimensional drawing becomes available.

### Gaps
- Exact local lid thickness, rear curvature, corner radii, camera/notch coordinates, Apple-logo position/surface texture, bezel and glass offsets are not published in the sources found.
- No official public A3112 STEP/IGES or dimensioned lid drawing was located in the bounded Apple support/accessory, web and step.parts checks. This is “not located in bounded search,” not a claim of nonexistence.
- Community STEP model source, underlying measurement method, and redistribution license remain unverified; do not download/use it as a manufacturing reference without that review.

## Mounting interface, safety, and retract/detach choice

### Takeaway
For a removable accessory, place the load path on a replaceable case or sacrificial skin, then use a small detachable dock-and-arm assembly; a purely behind-lid slider may stow compactly but adds track, latch and travel-clearance work. Do not assume magnets adhere to bare aluminum or that an adhesive is safe on Apple's finish without direct material testing.

### Cited Findings
- Apple describes a large flat glass display and aluminum enclosure for the M4 generation on iFixit's model reference; the current Apple specifications identify the model and published dimensions, but neither source certifies any third-party adhesive, magnetic mount, clamp, or load on the lid. [iFixit model page](https://www.ifixit.com/Device/MacBook_Pro_14%22_Late_2024_M4), [Apple specifications](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/121552)
- Apple's repair documentation treats the display as a service assembly and provides dedicated display repair steps; it is evidence that the display contains service-sensitive parts, not permission to clamp or press against glass. [Apple M4 repair manual](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/120922)
- Apple notes MacBook Pro may become warm in normal use and should remain on a stable, ventilated surface while in use or charging. This cautions against covering vents or adding a hot-running electronic load over sensitive areas; it does not specify a lid temperature limit. [Apple product information](https://cdsassets.apple.com/live/6GJYWVAV/information/locale/es-mx/macbook-pro-14inch-m4-2024-info.pdf)
- Apple gives MagSafe and port locations in the overall product specification; side/top edge placement should be reviewed against the actual unit and hinge opening range. [Apple technical specifications](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/121552)

### Inferences
- Use a mount bonded to a replaceable hard case or skin when feasible. A low-profile, wide-backed detachable dock with a positive mechanical arm catch is a conservative first concept: remove the pod/arm for a bag and leave the small dock protected behind the lid. A skin is still a consumable surface and needs peel/residue testing; a hard case spreads force but may increase the lid envelope and alter fit.
- Direct adhesive on anodized aluminum is a higher-risk alternative: surface finish, residue, cleaning chemistry, dwell time, temperature and peel direction all affect removal. Test coupon on a representative finished aluminum coupon or sacrificial skin before any laptop use; do not claim residue-free removal.
- Avoid pressure on display glass, bezel, camera, hinge covers and lid edge; avoid any clamp that squeezes lid thickness. Confirm lid closure, hinge sweep, sleep/wake, camera/FOV and ports with accessory installed. Do not rely on unknown magnets to attach to aluminum; if magnets are used for a detachable joint, fully capture them mechanically and test for interference with sensors and nearby devices.
- A slider stows the head behind the lid but requires a guide, end stop and accidental-deployment retention. Its stowed travel envelope is at least the arm's deployed reach plus head envelope along the chosen axis; exact package size cannot be calculated until the face diameter, arm length, track length and lid bounds are fixed. A detachable arm/pod can be stored separately and reduces permanently exposed parts, at the cost of a loose component and dock interface. A fold hinge adds local thickness and needs a reliable retention feature; leave it out until a specific bag envelope demands it.
- Provisional static example only: a 20 g pod whose center of mass is 50 mm from the mount gives gravitational moment `0.020 kg × 9.81 m/s² × 0.050 m = 0.00981 N·m` (9.81 mN·m). This ignores arm mass, acceleration, impact, adhesive peel and stress concentration, so it is not an allowable hinge or adhesive rating. Measure assembled mass and reach, then apply an explicit dynamic factor and test on a fixture.

### Gaps
- No verified allowable accessory mass or hinge torque for the laptop was found. Apple's overall specs and service manual do not certify added hinge load.
- Lid opening angle, hinge sweep/torque, precise local mounting shape and closure clearance must be checked on the user's physical laptop.
- Actual connector, display board, cable bend radius, battery and compute package dimensions are not available in this assignment. No internal electronics or wiring layout is included here.

## Measurement and printable prototype plan

### Takeaway
Measure before making the mating face: take local thickness and surface readings at the intended point, build fit coupons before a full dock, and test the mechanism with a dummy mass before adding electronics. PETG is a practical first functional print for a small indoor prototype; ASA is a better candidate for warmer exposure when the printer/process supports it, while PLA is best kept to quick appearance/fit mocks.

### Cited Findings
- Prusa describes PETG as suitable for technical/mechanical parts, low-warping and tough, and recommends adding perimeters rather than relying only on infill for solid parts; its guidance says PETG parts suit interiors and most exteriors below 80 °C. These are material-family/vendor guidelines, not guarantees for a particular filament, print orientation or part geometry. [Prusa PETG guide](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/petg_2059)
- Prusa's material comparison identifies PETG as temperature resistant and ASA as a material for which an enclosure is recommended; its guide offers material-specific print settings and qualitative limitations. Actual settings depend on printer and filament maker. [Prusa filament material guide](https://help.prusa3d.com/filament-material-guide)
- Bambu Lab lists the H2S as an FDM printer with 340 × 320 × 340 mm build volume, a 350 °C maximum nozzle temperature, a 0.4 mm included hardened-steel nozzle, and supported diameters of 0.2/0.4/0.6/0.8 mm; PETG and PLA are listed as supported materials. These are capability limits, not recommended settings for this small part. [Bambu Lab H2S specifications](https://bambulab.com/it/support/buying-guide)
- Bambu Studio's official profile source includes a dedicated `Bambu Lab H2S 0.4 nozzle` machine profile, default `0.20mm Standard @BBL H2S` process, and H2S-compatible Bambu PLA Basic and PETG HF filament profiles. This confirms a native profile exists in the upstream profile set; it cannot confirm what version/profile is currently selected on the user's installed slicer. [H2S 0.4 machine profile](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bambulab/BambuStudio/master/resources/profiles/BBL/machine/Bambu%20Lab%20H2S%200.4%20nozzle.json), [H2S 0.20 Standard process](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bambulab/BambuStudio/master/resources/profiles/BBL/process/0.20mm%20Standard%20@BBL%20H2S.json), [H2S Bambu PLA Basic preset](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bambulab/BambuStudio/master/resources/profiles/BBL/filament/Bambu%20PLA%20Basic%20@BBL%20H2S.json), [H2S Bambu PETG HF preset](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bambulab/BambuStudio/master/resources/profiles/BBL/filament/Bambu%20PETG%20HF%20@BBL%20H2S.json)
- In FDM, part strength depends on print orientation and layer adhesion; a specific strength/creep rating cannot be inferred from generic polymer names alone. No validated load data for this proposed mount or printer/material combination was found in the sources reviewed.

### Inferences
- Measurement checklist at the exact intended attachment site:
  1. Identify model/finish and mark the intended anchor point relative to Apple logo, top/side edges and camera.
  2. Measure lid total thickness at the edge and proposed center point with a low-force caliper; do not clamp the caliper against the display. Use a depth probe/straightedge or non-contact profile method for back curvature and local flatness.
  3. Record the nearby corner radius, edge-to-camera/notch and edge-to-logo distances, glass/bezel boundary, rubber/hinge covers, antenna/vent areas and any seam or lip.
  4. With an unpowered dummy mount, check lid opening and closing through full travel, closed-lid gap, display-side clearance, camera view, screen edge clearance, port access and cable flex. Recheck after installing the chosen case/skin.
  5. Record the finish (silver/Space Black, matte/skin/case) and test any adhesive/liner peel on a spare equivalent surface. Use a coupon over several days and warm/cool cycles before committing.
- First build uses one discreet combined power/data cable. Select a single cable/connector only after confirming the display/controller board actually carries both power and data over that connection (USB-C is an option only if the specific boards support it). Record connector body and latch clearance, cable diameter, and the cable maker's minimum bend radius before routing. Sketch the route from pod to controller/power along the rear lid toward the hinge, with a flexible service loop clear of hinge knuckles and pinch lines; open and close the lid slowly through full travel while watching the loop and connector strain relief. Do not run a taut cable across the hinge axis or between display and base where it can be pinched. Keep the cable removable and provide slack/retention at pod and dock; exact loop length depends on the selected cable and measured route.
- Keep first-fit geometry simple and explicit: nominal interface pad/wall of about 1.5–2.0 mm and clearance increments of 0.2/0.4/0.6 mm are suggested *coupon starting values only*, not measured fit tolerances or validated printer compensation. On the H2S, first confirm installed nozzle diameter/material and select the matching H2S machine variant in Bambu Studio. For the standard included 0.4 mm nozzle, use its native `Bambu Lab H2S 0.4 nozzle` profile and `0.20mm Standard @BBL H2S` process; use the matching filament-maker preset, or the Bambu PLA Basic / PETG HF H2S preset only when using those actual filaments. Print same-orientation small pad/rail and clearance coupons at 0.2/0.4/0.6 mm fit variants in both PETG and PLA, keeping all other slicer settings fixed. Compare fit and retention by hand on a sacrificial backing/case, then update CAD from measurements. Use a thin compliant liner only if characterized and shown not to migrate or mark the surface. Do not import an H2S profile into a different printer/nozzle selection or infer a custom filament profile from a generic polymer name.
- Start coupons with the H2S standard 0.4 mm nozzle and ordinary 0.20 mm layer-height process. Use PLA for appearance and quick dimensional checks; test PETG for the working dock/arm coupon. Match the profile to the spool maker/material, keep default speeds/temperatures initially, and do not use the H2S maximum nozzle or chamber capability as a target. The profile's 0.20 mm layer setting is an upstream starting preset, not a fit-tolerance guarantee.
- Prototype sequence: (1) caliper/profile record and paper outline; (2) flat/curved fit coupons; (3) dummy dock/arm with pod-mass surrogate and rounded accessible edges; (4) bench retention and peel/slip tests on sacrificial representative surface; (5) low-speed, hand-operated deployment/removal; (6) full opening/closing and bag-envelope check; (7) breadboard real display/electronics only after the mechanics pass; (8) final enclosure and repeated cycle/transport checks. Keep electronics out of early stress/fit tests.
- For a projecting arm, orient the print so the arm root is not loaded by pulling apart layer planes; use fillets and broad root transitions. Use multiple perimeters in the root and hinge cheeks, with infill only to support the remaining section. A metal pin/shoulder screw through printed hinge cheeks is preferable to a thin printed pin if repeated cycling is required. Keep the face shell independent/removable so it can be replaced without removing the dock.
- A reasonable first material choice is PETG for the small non-hot indoor functional mock, with a low-cost PLA appearance body to validate visual placement. Consider ASA if the mount may be left in a warm vehicle or sunlit environment and the printer has enclosure/ventilation capability; confirm the exact filament's data and settings. Avoid sustained clamp preload in PLA and do not interpret material labels as creep certification.
- Fit/stack budget should be parameterized, not frozen: `total rear protrusion = measured back-surface offset + attachment interface + dock base + slider/fold stack + stowed head/arm envelope`. As a nonbinding paper example only, if coupons later indicate a 0.5 mm liner, a 2 mm dock wall and 3 mm rail stack, then the dock region projects about 5.5 mm before any head; these are illustrative estimates, not a MacBook-specific clearance promise.

### Gaps
- Printer model, nozzle, material brand, layer height, measured assembled electronics mass, chosen face dimensions, arm reach, and selected dock location are unknown, so no final wall thickness, tolerance, infill, load rating, print orientation or closed-bag envelope can yet be certified.
- Actual fit, adhesive compatibility, hinge load, glass clearance, heat exposure, long-term creep, cycle life and bag snag/impact safety remain untested.
- A functional CAD revision should wait for the measurement record and selected mechanism; this note intentionally does not change or certify the exploratory CAD.
